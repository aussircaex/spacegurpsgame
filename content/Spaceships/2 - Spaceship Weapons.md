##### On Beam Weapons vs. Ballistic Weapons
  In interpersonal combat, ballistic guns are still widely used, due to the difficulty and expense of miniaturizing beam weapon technologies. For ship-mounted weaponry that can be fed external power, however, energy weapons are far superior. The distances involved in space combat tend to make ballistic cannon fire impractical for ship-to-ship, even with the electromagnetic Gauss technology available at that scale. However, due to the fact that ballistic cannons are not power-hungry systems, they are sometimes employed by civilian craft or the defense forces of lower-GDP worlds as point-defense weaponry, where the velocity and range of the cannons are mitigated.
### Weapon Types
#### Laser Cannons, Blaster Cannons, and Turbolasers
  Variously utilizing laser beams, particle beams, and more recently, anti-particle beams, these weapons are highly accurate and long-range. Their performance, while varied, all fall close enough to one another to use the same stats. The three terms are used for progressively larger weapons, but the lines between them are subjective.
  
  *Statistics:* treat as "Heat Beam" for damage, accuracy, recoil and range.

#### Plasma Cannons, Pulse Cannons
  Due to the Universal Union's ability to blockade munitions production from resupplying Terran warships, humanity adapted this technology as an alternative. Short-range and difficulty to aim, these heavy weapons fire green-colored globules of high-energy plasma which deal as much as double damage compared to laser fire, and causes explosions on hits. Designated "bomber" craft often mount these cannons instead of carrying missiles or torpedoes. Humanity later developed Pulse Cannons, which operate similarly but produce smaller spheres of plasma that can be fired more rapidly (*narratively, if it's a smaller or rapid-fire battery, call it a Pulse Cannon*)
  
  *Statistics:* treat as "Plasma Beam" for damage, accuracy, recoil and range; ignore its armor divisor.

#### Gauss Cannons, Railguns
  Electromagnets allow for ship-based conventional weapons to achieve very high velocities, but are still considered very impractical for ship-to-ship combat, especially on larger vessels, as their damage does not scale as well as ship defenses do. Nevertheless, they are somewhat commonplace on civilian craft as short-range defensive weaponry, especially when a ship's reactor does not have the power to fuel high-energy beam weaponry.
  
  *Statistics:* Treat as "Electromagnetic Gun" for accuracy and recoil. Treat as "Particle Beam" for range. Ignore relative velocity. (Note that the damage is based off the gun caliber, not the beam weapon damage dice.)

#### Missiles, Torpedoes, Bombs
  Modern ballistic missiles may contain high-energy payloads such as plasma rather than conventional explosives, but the effects are the same. Missiles deal high damage, but take time to reach their targets, and point defense weaponry or fighter screens can intercept them.

  To attack with a missile, a ship takes an Attack action as normal and launches its salvo, but does not make any attack rolls yet. Missiles are placed on the hex map at the same location as the attacker, with the same vector. They have a Move stat of 10 and 30 Burn Points (meaning they can move their vector 30 points total), always try to set their vector on their target's hex, and always choose their vector last in the turn. When they reach their target's hex, the attack roll is resolved as normal.

  The missiles can be attacked like any normal target; any hit destroys one missile. Point-defense weaponry can be used proactively, or use the "Wait (Point Defense)" action to allow the missiles to reach a range of 0 before firing (the Size Modifier of a missile is on SS47).

  *Statistics:* Treat as "Missile Launcher (20-28cm)" or "Missile Launcher (32cm+)" as appropriate for accuracy and recoil. Use the "Launchers" damage appropriate to the battery (which is usually 2x the size of a gun in the same slot). For range, treat as a "Heat Ray" three sizes larger.
    *Example: a SM+5 bomber firing a salvo of torpedoes from its Major Battery (10MJ-slot) is firing 20cm warheads that will deal 6dx5 damage, and have the range of a 300MJ Heat Ray, or 2/5 hexes. Once out of ammo, it will close in to fire its Major Battery Plasma Cannons that can deal 8d damage per hit, but their range is 0/1 by comparison!
##### On Statistics
  1. For any given spaceship weapon, the "baseline" statistics are found in *GURPS Spaceships* under the various Batteries (SS26-28) and the Weapon Tables (SS67-68), as appropriate to the ship's size and the type of battery. If a weapon is noted as doing extra damage, it is a multiplier of this baseline. For example, if a Plasma Cannon does "twice the damage", then choosing it as a Medium Battery for your SM+8 ship means it deals 6dx2 damage (this is also found in the "dDam2" column on SS67).
  2. To find the Range of a weapon, check *GURPS Spaceships 3: Warships and Space Pirates*, page 37-39. We are using the "1000 mile" column. Unless otherwise stated, Laser Cannons, Blaster Cannons, and Turbolasers use the "Heat Ray" table, and Plasma Cannons and Pulse Cannons use the "Plasma Beam" table.
  3. Some ships have a Gravitic Focus installed, allowing a fixed frontal Major Battery or a Spinal Mount battery to fire at 10x the given range for the weapon, but at 1/10 of the damage. Powering such a system requires an extra Power Point, and so they are most frequently utilized for the "warning shots" that often initiate an ambush.
